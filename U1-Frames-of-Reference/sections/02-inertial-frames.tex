\section{Inertial frames of reference}

	A reference frame (e.g. a coordinate system) in which Newton's first law of motion holds good, is known as an \emph{inertial frame of reference}. Hence, in an inertial frame a body continues in its state of rest or
	of uniform motion as long as no external force acts on it.
	
	All frames of reference moving with a constant velocity with respect to an inertial frame are also inertial frames of reference.
	
	\begin{figure}[h!]
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=0.35\textwidth]{figures/fig_inertial-frame-01.png}
	\end{figure}
	
	Consider an inertial frame of reference $S$ and another reference
	frame $S'$, such that their orientations in space remain fixed
	and are thus free to have only translatory motion with respect to
	one another. Suppose further that the origins $O$ and $O'$ at the
	two systems initially coincident. 
	
	As shown in the figure the frame $S'$ has moved into its current position in time $t$ with a constant velocity $\vb{v}$ relative to $S$. Then we have
	\begin{align*}
		\bar{OO'}=\vb{R}=\vb{v}t
	\end{align*}
	The position vectors $\vb{r}$ and $\vb{r}'$ of a particle $P$, with respect to the two frames of reference are then related as
	\begin{align*}
		\vb{r}&=\vb{R}+\vb{r}' \\
		\implies\vb{r}'&=\vb{r}-\vb{R} \\
		\implies\vb{r}'&=\vb{r}-\vb{v}t
	\end{align*}
	
	Differentiating the above, we get
	\begin{align*}
		\dv{\vb{r}'}{t}&=\dv{\vb{r}}{t}-\vb{v}
	\end{align*}
	
	Differentiating once again, we get
	\begin{align*}
		\dv[2]{\vb{r}'}{t}&=\dv[2]{\vb{r}}{t} \\
		\implies\vb{a}'&=\vb{a}
	\end{align*}
	
	Hence, the acceleration of the particle is the same in the two frames of reference. This indicates that if the particle is at rest or uniform motion in the frame $S$, then it would also appear to be at rest or uniform motion in the other frame $S'$.
	
	\emph{Therefore, all frames of reference, moving with a constant velocity with respect to an inertial frame, are also inertial frames of reference.}
	
	\textbf{Examples of inertial frames of reference:}
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item An object in deep space, away from any gravitational influence, can be considered to be in an inertial frame of reference.
		
		\item An object falling freely under the influence of gravity can be considered to be in an inertial frame of reference.
	\end{enumerate}
